2. Problem Statement & Pune City Urban Context

Pune is recognized as one of India's foremost urban hubs for renewable energy adoption. However, project developers, cooperative housing societies, and industrial plant managers face substantial structural bottlenecks:

  • Climatic Vulnerability: Pune experiences high pre-monsoon solar irradiation (~5.5 kWh/m²/day) with ambient temperatures exceeding 42°C, causing severe thermal degradation in low-grade PV modules. This is followed by heavy south-west monsoon precipitation (June–September) requiring stringent IP65/IP66 inverter ingress protection, and winter dust accumulation demanding high soiling tolerance.
  • Fragmented Technical Information: International equipment specifications (temperature coefficients, degradation rates, inverter MPPT ranges) are scattered across corporate marketing brochures with zero verified provenance.
  • Local Service Footprint Ambiguity: Many global manufacturers claim presence in India but lack authorized physical service centers, technical engineers, or spare parts warehouses in Maharashtra.
  • MSEDCL Net-Metering Compliance: Under Maharashtra Electricity Regulatory Commission (MERC) regulations, rooftop systems must comply with strict sanctioned load ceilings (100% of contract demand), anti-islanding relay specifications, and three-phase meter transitions for capacities above 10 kW.
